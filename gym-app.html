UX Case study · 2025

LiftIQ: a gym app with an AI form coach

Most beginner lifters don't have a trainer. They have YouTube, conflicting advice, and a real chance of getting hurt. LiftIQ pairs an AI coach that checks your form in real time with short videos and written breakdowns — so you learn the lift instead of guessing.

The problem

Gym-goers without a trainer get little guidance on form and technique — which raises injury risk and stalls progress.

What I did

Researched, designed, and iteratively tested an AI coach paired with multimedia instruction.

The result

A mobile experience that combines real-time AI motion tracking, instructional video, and written resources.

01 Background & pain points

Improper form and ineffective training techniques are common among beginner to intermediate gym-goers (ages 16–30), especially those without access to a personal trainer. The most common problems:

  • Injury risk from incorrect exercise execution.
  • Confusion from conflicting fitness information online.
  • No feedback loop for tracking progression and form improvements over time.

Personally relevant, too: I've spent more hours researching lifting technique (and the “optimal” split) than I'd like to admit.

02 Competitive analysis

I surveyed top fitness apps — Nike Training Club, Peloton, and Fitbod — and found that none offered integrated, AI-driven form correction. That gap opened an opportunity for real-time, motion-tracking feedback.

03 UX research

Responsibilities

  • Initial concept design grounded in fitness science.
  • Detailed user tests and contextual inquiries.
  • Analyzing feedback for iterative improvements.

How I collected data

  • Think-aloud protocol while people used the prototypes.
  • Qualitative interviews about fitness habits, challenges, and app use.
  • Competitive review of leading fitness apps.
  • Expert interviews with 2 certified trainers.

How I analyzed it

  • Thematic analysis to find patterns and recurring usability issues.
  • Direct feedback integration for user-centered design changes.

04 Design & iteration

Three rounds, each one shaped by what testers actually struggled with.

v0 · initial prototype

The core loop

  • AI coach with real-time camera analysis and audio feedback.
  • Video walkthroughs with descriptive captions and form cues.
  • Progress tracking and detailed instructional articles.

Testers loved the AI coach, the clean UI, and the videos — but kept getting lost thanks to small, inconsistent back buttons.

v1 · mid-fidelity

Fixing navigation, deepening instruction

  • Enlarged and standardized back/navigation buttons.
  • Added step-by-step breakdowns (setup, execution, correction) based on safety concerns.
  • Customizable playback speed for instructional videos.

Satisfaction went up with the more consistent navigation and extra interactivity.

v2 · high-fidelity

The final design

  • Advanced AI feedback with highlighted visual cues.
  • Expanded resources on proper form and hypertrophy technique.
  • Better progress logging for measurable improvement.
  • A consistent interface across every screen.

05 The learning science behind it

LiftIQ leans on established principles from educational psychology:

  • Mayer's multimedia principlesVisual (video, highlighted muscles) plus audio coaching uses both channels and reduces cognitive overload.
  • Embodied cognitionLearning by physically doing the movement strengthens memory and skill acquisition.
  • Immediate feedbackReal-time AI form corrections reduce risk and speed up learning.
  • ChunkingComplex lifts are broken into setup, execution, and correction.
  • SignalingVisual cues direct attention to exactly what needs fixing.

06 Reflection & next steps

Breaking exercises into setup, execution, and correction phases turned out to be critical for learning. Iterative testing also made it obvious that consistent navigation matters more than any single feature — hence the bigger back buttons. Next, I'd:

  • Collect quantitative usability data (error rates, completion times).
  • Work with physical therapists to tune the AI coach's thresholds.
  • Explore adaptive difficulty based on performance trends.
