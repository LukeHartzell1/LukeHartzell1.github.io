Community Stories for Beelinguapp

Beelinguapp teaches languages through side-by-side stories, but learners told us those stories didn't reflect them. Our team designed Community Stories: a way for learners to share and discover culturally relevant stories, grounded in research on inequality in language education.

01 Background & pain points

Beelinguapp offers stories in a target language alongside a translation in the learner's home language, tagged by difficulty. But it falls short on personalization and cultural depth. Learners, and English learners (EL students) in particular, ran into:

  • A lack of culturally relevant storylines.
  • No community-driven content or user-generated cultural narratives.
  • Little context around stories, so engagement stayed shallow.

Those gaps fed disengagement and stereotype threat, both of which hurt language acquisition and motivation.

02 Competitive analysis

We reviewed the leading language-learning apps (Duolingo, Memrise, and LingQ) and found none offering true community-driven storytelling or deep cultural context. That gap directly shaped our feature set and priorities.

04 UX research

My responsibilities

  • Designing an initial concept grounded in language-inequality research.
  • Running iterative user testing to refine usability and inclusivity.
  • Analyzing feedback with affinity diagrams to pinpoint key insights.

How we collected data

  • Contextual inquiries with diverse bilingual users.
  • Think-aloud usability tests on the mid-fidelity prototype.
  • Competitive review of Duolingo, Memrise, and LingQ.
  • Interviews with 3 language-instructor stakeholders.
  • Heuristic evaluation of existing community-sharing workflows.

How we analyzed it

  • Qualitative thematic analysis of user feedback.
  • Affinity diagramming to cluster insights into actionable improvements.

05 Exploring the problem space

To ground the design in real needs, I led an affinity-diagramming workshop that synthesized our contextual inquiries and think-aloud sessions. It helped us cluster quotes and observations into themes, surface recurring pain points around cultural relevance and engagement, and spot the biggest opportunities for community storytelling. The resulting map became our north star for prioritization.

06 The low-fidelity wireframe

My first sketch focused on the core Community Stories flow:

  • Placeholder cards for each story.
  • Basic navigation tabs (“All Stories” / “My Stories”).
  • A simple “Add Story” button in the top-right.
  • A minimal legend for metadata (date, author, language).

This low-fi pass let us validate the information hierarchy before moving to mid-fi mockups.

07 Design solutions & iteration

v0, initial prototype

A home for community stories

  • A community story page with a basic “Add Story” feature and search.
  • Preliminary filtering for community-generated content.

Users liked having a dedicated community page and search, but filtering felt thin and story context was unclear.

v1, mid-fidelity

Search that keeps up

  • Live search.
  • A Community Preview on the homepage to drive early engagement.

Feedback asked for more filtering, clearer story context, and a better-presented preview component.

v2, high-fidelity

The final design

  • Advanced filters (submission date, popularity).
  • A carousel for the homepage preview.
  • Context everywhere: tags, short descriptions, and synopses.
  • Verification and confirmation states for story uploads.
v2, upload flow

Trust and safety, built in

Story uploads go through a verification step with clear confirmation states, so contributors know exactly where their story stands.

08 Why it works: learning science

  • Linguistic inclusivitySupports multilingualism with content in diverse dialects and languages, closing content gaps.
  • Counter-narratives & identityLets marginalized learners share affirming, culturally relevant stories, which pushes back on stereotype threat.
  • Sociocultural learningEncourages peer engagement and social learning through community contributions.
  • Recognition over recallFamiliar patterns and consistent standards keep the experience intuitive for diverse learners.

10 Reflection & next steps

Iterative testing was indispensable: every cycle surfaced both wins and new pain points. The filtering feature got strong praise, and standardizing our Figma frames made the layouts far more consistent. Going forward, I'd:

  • Gather quantitative metrics (SUS, task-completion rates) on the next prototype.
  • Bring in more language-education experts to refine the cultural content.
  • Explore community moderation tools to keep quality high.

If I could change one thing about the project, I'd front-load the Figma workshops and give user testing a longer window to fit people's schedules.
